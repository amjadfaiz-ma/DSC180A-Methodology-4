## Quarter 2 Project Reflection

**1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

The most interesting topic to me is how emotions can influence the behavior and decision-making of large language models (LLMs). Our project, E-STEER, explores how emotions can be represented through three dimensions: Valence, Arousal, and Dominance (VAD). I find it interesting that we can manipulate these emotional representations within a model's internal activations and observe how they affect its responses without retraining the model.

**2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

For Quarter 2, I would like to investigate how different emotional dimensions affect the reasoning and decision-making of LLMs. Specifically, I would like to examine whether adjusting Valence, Arousal, or Dominance produces consistent changes in model behavior across different tasks. This could help us better understand which emotional dimensions have the greatest influence on model responses.

**3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

One change I would make is to expand our experiments beyond the smaller model and limited evaluation questions used in our initial proof-of-concept. Our current baseline uses a 0.5B parameter model and a small set of reasoning questions, which limits how much we can conclude from the results. For Quarter 2, I would like to focus on testing the larger 8B model and using the paper's original methodology to see whether the findings remain consistent.

**4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in using ablation studies and visualizations of the model's internal activations. Ablation studies could help us identify which emotional features contribute most to changes in the model's behavior. Visualizing these activations could also help us better understand how different emotional dimensions are represented and how they influence the model's responses.
